# How overtime is counted under a fixed working hours system

Statutory overtime work is judged on both a **daily** and a **weekly** basis (Article 32 of the Labor
Standards Act).

## Order of the judgment

1. **Daily judgment** — the actual working hours of that day minus 8 hours is that day's statutory overtime work
2. **Weekly judgment** — the hours that did not become overtime in step 1 (work within statutory working hours)
   are accumulated from the start of the week, and the hours exceeding 40 are added to statutory overtime work

**This order matters.** Judging by the week first would put work that has already been counted as overtime for
exceeding 8 hours in a day into the weekly total as well, counting the same work twice.

### Example: working 6 days a week, 7 hours a day

Each day is within 8 hours, so the daily judgment in step 1 produces no overtime.
The weekly total, however, is 42 hours, so the **2 hours** in excess of 40 hours are weekly statutory overtime work.

### Example: working 5 days a week, with 10 hours on just one of them

The 10-hour day produces **2 hours** of daily statutory overtime.
The remaining work within statutory working hours for the week is 8 hours × 4 days + 8 hours = exactly 40 hours,
so nothing is added on the weekly judgment. The total is 2 hours.

## The day the week starts

Judging the 40-hour week requires a week boundary. Where the rules of employment do not provide for one,
a week **starting on Sunday** is the rule (昭和63年基発第1号, Notification Kihatsu No. 1 of 1988).
In KIZAMI this can be set per company.

## The 8-hour day does not change at a 特例措置対象事業場

For workplaces in commerce, the film and theater industry, health and hygiene services, or the hospitality
and entertainment industry that regularly employ fewer than 10 workers
(特例措置対象事業場, a workplace covered by the special measure), the statutory weekly working hours are
relaxed to 44 hours (Article 40 of the Labor Standards Act and Article 25-2 of the Ordinance for Enforcement
of the Labor Standards Act). **Only the week is relaxed; the 8 hours per day do not move.**

## Weeks that span two months (KIZAMI behavior)

For a week that spans two months, KIZAMI judges the 40-hour week using **only the days that fall within that
month's period**. Hours from the previous month are not carried over.

This behavior gives priority to keeping the figures of a closed month from moving afterwards. As a result,
weekly statutory overtime work can come out lower than it actually was in the first week of a month. Where
long working hours span the start of a month, check the daily actual working hours in the monthly list as well.
