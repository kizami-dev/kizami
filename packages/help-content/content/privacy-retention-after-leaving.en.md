# Retention and erasure of records after leaving

Leaving the company does not mean that the attendance records disappear straight away. This is
because **the law requires them to be retained**.

## Two laws point in opposite directions

| Law | What it requires |
| --- | --- |
| Article 109 of the Labor Standards Act | Records such as the wage ledger and the attendance record book **must be retained** (five years in principle; three years for the time being under the transitional measure in Article 143(2) of the supplementary provisions) |
| Article 22 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information | Where personal data no longer needs to be used, the operator **must endeavor to erase it without delay** |

For a person who has left, these two collide head-on: erasing the records goes against the Labor
Standards Act, while keeping them goes against the duty to endeavor under the Act on the Protection
of Personal Information.

Since the duty to retain is a "must" (a mandatory duty) while erasure is a "must endeavor to"
(a duty to endeavor), the correct order is to **keep the records until the retention period has run
out**.

## When the retention period starts to run

The retention period under Article 109 of the Labor Standards Act is counted from "the date of the
last entry". For a person who has left, the last attendance record is that date. KIZAMI treats **the
date on which the leaving process was carried out** as the starting date. Because the leaving
process takes place on or after the last day worked, this treatment can only fall **later** than the
actual starting date, never earlier, and so it never cuts into the statutory period.

## What happens once the period has passed

Once the retention period has passed, an administrator becomes able to erase the personal data of
the person who has left. What disappears and what remains is as follows.

| Item | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Name and email address | Replaced with a form that does not identify the individual |
| Password, two-factor authentication, and login information | Deleted |
| Notification settings and device information (IP address, browser information, location information) | Deleted |
| Attendance records (punch times, aggregated results, figures for closed months) | **Kept** (because of the retention duty) |
| Audit log | **Kept** (not altered; only the name shown in it is anonymized) |

In other words, this is not "erasing the records" but **removing from the records the information
about whose records they are**. Because the attendance record rows themselves are not deleted, the
aggregated figures for months that have already been closed are the same before and after the
erasure.

This operation **cannot be undone**. If you would like your own records disclosed to you after
leaving, contact the company's point of contact before the retention period runs out.

## The company chooses the retention period

Whether to adopt three years (the transitional measure) or five years (the principle) is for the
company to decide. The KIZAMI default is **five years** — the transitional measure will end at some
point, and a company that has left the setting at three years would no longer meet the retention
duty at that time.
