# The total hours limit under a flexible working hours system

Under a flexible working hours system, working hours are judged not day by day but on the **total for the
settlement period (one month with this setting)**. The upper limit of hours to be worked in that period is
called the total working hours limit (総枠).

```
Total hours limit = statutory weekly working hours × calendar days in that month ÷ 7
```

Statutory weekly working hours are 40 hours as a rule. By way of exception they are 44 hours for workplaces
in commerce, the film and theater industry, health and hygiene services, or the hospitality and entertainment
industry that regularly employ fewer than 10 workers
([特例措置対象事業場](./tenant-special-provision) — a workplace covered by the special measure).

| Days in the month | At 40 hours per week | At 44 hours per week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 days | 171 hours 25 minutes | 188 hours 34 minutes |
| 31 days | 177 hours 8 minutes | 194 hours 51 minutes |

Hours actually worked in excess of the total hours limit are **overtime work**, and hours below it are a
**shortfall**. They are shown as the "Flex balance" on the KIZAMI monthly screen.

## Total working hours for the settlement period and the statutory limit

The labor-management agreement sets the **total working hours in the settlement period** — the hours to be
worked in that period (Article 32-3(1)(ii) of the Labor Standards Act). It must be set within the total hours
limit above (the **statutory limit**); total working hours exceeding the statutory limit cannot be set
(Labour Standards Bureau Notice No. 1 of January 1, 1988).

When the total working hours are set shorter than the statutory limit (for example, for reduced working hours
for childcare or family care), actual hours fall into three bands.

| Band | Range | Premium wages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Within the total working hours | Up to the total working hours | Not required |
| Excess within the statutory limit | Beyond the total working hours, up to the statutory limit | Not required (wages for the hours worked are still due) |
| Statutory overtime work | Beyond the statutory limit | Required (Article 37 of the Labor Standards Act) |

For how KIZAMI decides the total working hours, see [How total working hours are decided under the flexible working hours system, and carrying over shortfalls](./attendance-flex-contract).

## Carrying over shortfalls and handling excess hours

When actual hours fall short of the total working hours, settling the shortfall by **adding it to the total
working hours of the next settlement period** is not considered unlawful, **as long as the result stays within
the statutory limit** (Labour Standards Bureau Notice No. 1 of January 1, 1988).

On the other hand, hours worked beyond the total working hours (an excess) cannot be carried over to the next
settlement period to offset it. Doing so would mean not paying wages for work in that settlement period as
wages for that period, which violates the **principle of full payment of wages** (Article 24 of the Labor
Standards Act).

## How days of paid leave are treated

A day on which annual paid leave is taken counts toward the actual hours as if it had been worked (the
prescribed working hours for a full day, half of that for a half day, and the corresponding hours for hourly
units). Taking paid leave never increases the shortfall.
